# AEVO teaching framework — proposed paper outline

**Status: Conceptual / proposal, not a completed pedagogical manuscript or evaluated instructional intervention.**

## Educational purpose

Design a teachable approach to AI-assisted scientific inquiry in which students develop ownership of the **scientific question and quality criteria**, rather than confounding fluent generated output with trustworthy research.

## Four roles as assessable student capabilities

| Phase | Student competency | Example assessable artifact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Architect | Formulate a falsifiable question; specify scope, assumptions, conceptual model, uncertainty and validation criteria | Study plan, preregistered acceptance tests, conceptual diagram |
| Execute | Build a transparent workflow for data sourcing, computation, modelling and writing using appropriate AI tools | Reproducible scripts, provenance manifest, execution log |
| Verify | Independently test generated claims, methods, code, statistics and sensitivity to assumptions | Verification checklist, uncertainty analysis, red-team report |
| Own | Explain interpretation and limits; disclose AI contributions; defend conclusions and make responsible decisions | Signed research memo, oral defence, contributor disclosure |

## Pedagogical research questions (unanswered)

1. Does AEVO-based instruction improve students' independent verification accuracy relative to ordinary AI-assisted project work?
2. Does it improve research-question quality, reproducibility and calibrated confidence?
3. Can improvements be distinguished from differences in prior domain knowledge, coding ability, time on task and AI-model access?
4. Do gains persist when students work without AI on a transfer assessment?

## Evaluation sketch

- **Design:** pilot plus preregistered comparison study where feasible; ideally randomized sections or matched groups with suitable controls.
- **Primary outcomes:** blinded scoring of architecture artifacts, accuracy of error detection on deliberately flawed outputs, reproducibility success, and quality of justification.
- **Secondary outcomes:** self-efficacy, time expenditure, uncertainty communication, durable retention and independent transfer.
- **Safeguards:** instructor/student power dynamics; consent, privacy, accessibility, AI usage transparency, preregistration and limitations.
- **Important limitation:** no empirical effectiveness results or classroom outcome statistics are claimed here.

## Next steps

Survey relevant literature (project-based and inquiry learning, cognitive apprenticeship, metacognition, verification, AI literacy, research ethics); refine conceptual propositions; pilot rubrics; specify sampling, scoring reliability and analysis strategy; seek ethics review where appropriate.

**Relation to the position paper:** AEVO is a short educational corollary in the environmental-modelling perspective. This is a distinct pedagogical research track, with independent evidence and publication requirements.
